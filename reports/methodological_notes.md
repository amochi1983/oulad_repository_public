# Interpretation and limitations

This is a landmark observational study. No intervention effect, causal engagement effect, or reduction in socioeconomic inequality is identified. Engagement may reflect motivation, prior preparation, health, study time, course design and offline activity. Clicks are an imperfect proxy. Requiring survival without recorded withdrawal to each cutoff conditions on a changing population; week comparisons are not within-person temporal effects.

Registration missingness excludes enrolments. Withdrawn records with no unregistration date remain eligible when registration is known because the date cannot be invented; exclusions of all outcome/date inconsistencies form a sensitivity. Withdrawal on the cutoff day is not known in the date<c convention. Primary active proportion uses full landmark length; late registration can depress activity mechanically. Registration-lag adjustment is a sensitivity, not part of the primary specification.

IMD measures the residential area, not individual socioeconomic status. Missing IMD is an explicit separate group. Region and module/presentation adjustment cannot remove unmeasured confounding or resolve cross-national IMD comparability. Association-model fixed effects describe observed course presentations. Student-cluster sandwich intervals allow repeated enrolments but do not cluster module-level shocks; seven modules are insufficient for strong transportability conclusions. Wald tests, rather than a naive likelihood-ratio test ignoring clustered dependence, assess joint interaction coefficients. Descriptive Wilson CIs treat enrolments as independent; adjusted-model and predictive intervals account for students.

Probabilities are marginal standardisations over week-4 baseline covariates. Curves require overlapping group-specific 5th–95th percentile exposure support and at least 100 enrolments per group within ±0.10. This is marginal exposure support; high-dimensional covariate positivity is not established. CIs use delta-method gradients and cluster covariance conditional on the specification. Linear and fixed quadratic specifications are both reported; significance does not determine the primary specification.

Prospective models fit 2013 and evaluate 2014 students. Students in test are excluded from training, including other enrolments. Validation and calibration students are separate. Preprocessing is refit within the fitting partition; after tuning it is refit on fitting+validation students, with calibration and threshold training confined to a separate 2013 partition. Bootstrap CIs sample students and retain all their test enrolments, with 200 seeded draws. Paired contrasts share draws. Intervals condition on fitted models and do not propagate tuning/training variability or represent uncertainty across all possible modules. 2014 includes modules absent in 2013; unknown module encoding is all-zero with explicit interpretation and separate module summaries. B/J are observed presentation types; future year IDs are excluded.

Random forest and boosting use deliberately modest searches. Class weights/SMOTE are not needed for the moderately prevalent combined outcome; no resampling is performed. F1 thresholds are validation-policy choices; 0.5 remains the reference. Top-10/20% policies rank within each test cohort and break ties by stable row order; they are capacity-based rankings, not thresholds estimated from test outcomes. Subgroup metrics are policy-dependent and small cells unstable.

Alert-policy bootstrap intervals hold original alert membership fixed while resampling students; they estimate uncertainty conditional on the observed policy assignment, rather than re-ranking a fresh deployment cohort on each draw. Secondary outcome/assessment logistic sensitivities use fixed C=1 and uncalibrated outputs; their calibration results should not be compared as a calibrated-model contest. Leave-one-module-out tuning and calibration are rerun separately within each held-out module's training population; no globally selected boosting family is used to choose a module result.

Probability gaps can vary across engagement levels even when an interaction on the log-odds scale is not detected, because the logistic link is nonlinear. Distinguish association on the probability scale from the prespecified interaction test. Exact duplicate raw VLE rows are retained in primary counts; their origin is unidentifiable without event IDs. A separate exact-row-deduplicated click sensitivity is exported; distinct active days, the primary exposure, are invariant.

Secondary assessment scores have no release dates; any score-based estimate is an availability-limited sensitivity. Exams and banked assessments are excluded. Failure among non-withdrawn enrolments is conditional and should not be described as a population failure effect. Four-class exploratory outputs require adequate training counts.

SHAP explains predictive model outputs. Encoded IMD contributions and correlated engagement features are not intervention effects. First-order ALE is descriptive; redundant activity features limit single-feature interpretation. TreeSHAP interaction values are reported only after symmetry and sum-to-SHAP validation; see explanation_notes.md for actual execution status. The confirmatory regression interaction, fitted-tree interaction and first-order ALE answer distinct questions.
